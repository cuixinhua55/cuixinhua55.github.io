ABSTRACT: China's defense white papers have long proclaimed stable nuclear principles, yet debates over change in China's nuclear strategy persist. This article argues that the impasse reflects a methodological gap: existing studies focus on either declaratory commitments or material capabilities, but not on how official doctrine evolves as language. Using ten Chinese defense white papers (1998–2019), the study analyzes seven nuclear concepts through a mixed-methods design combining TF-IDF analysis and qualitative coding of verbs, modifiers, and actors. The findings show declaratory continuity alongside operational transformation. Declaratory principles remain highly standardized, while operational concepts shift toward military-organizational actors, capability and execution verbs, and increasingly technical modifiers. The reversal of nuclear counterattack relative to no-first-use in 2013 and 2015 provides direct evidence that the discursive emphasis on operational specification has increased. These results demonstrate that doctrinal stability can coexist with, and obscure, significant operational change in China's nuclear strategy.
