This article shows that operational nuclear thinking in China emerged well before nuclear acquisition. Drawing on two internal PLA documents from 1955, it demonstrates that nuclear weapons were systematically integrated into campaign-level planning, command organization, and combined-arms coordination. This finding challenges the prevailing view that early Chinese nuclear thinking was primarily political or deterrence-oriented. Rather than treating nuclear weapons as symbolic instruments, the PLA addressed the practical problem of how to fight under nuclear conditions at the level of operational execution prior to 1964. The evidence further shows that the gap between declaratory posture and operational logic was already present in the pre-acquisition period, highlighting the limitations of analyses based primarily on public sources.
